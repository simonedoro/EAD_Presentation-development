# The five challenges

Case material from the EAD challenge brief. Read the case for the participant's
challenge before you help them with anything.

**Use it in tiers. This matters.**

- **Tier 1, the brief.** Situation, complication, key facts, sources. This was given
  to every group. Pre-fill slides 3 and 4 with it. Tier 1b turns that same material
  into a stat slide, a chart and a list of image subjects. Use 1b by default: the
  numbers are the point, and they are wasted inside a sentence. Tell them it is pre-filled and ask
  them to change it if their work has moved on.
- **Tier 2, the question.** The first draft and the SMART version. The group writes
  their own. Use this to test theirs against the five tests and name what is missing.
  Show the model version only if they are stuck and ask for it, and say it is one
  possible version, not the answer.
- **Tier 3, the hypotheses.** The branches the problem splits into. Use these in
  Challenge mode to ask whether their three initiatives test different branches or
  crowd into one.
- **Tier 4, the model storyline.** Show only after they have written their own, as a
  comparison. Never before. Handing it over removes the exercise.

If a participant asks you outright for tiers 2 to 4, give it, but say what it costs
them: the jury scores their thinking, and this is the part the jury is scoring.

## 01 Waste segregation

Group 1. Sector: Environment Quality.

### Tier 1, the brief

**Slide 3, situation**
- Action title: Abu Dhabi is building the capacity to divert 80% of waste from landfill by 2031
- Statement: Fifty-one initiatives, new recovery facilities, a waste-to-energy plant and a bigger fleet are under way.
- Key facts: 37.7% of waste diverted from landfill in 2025 · 80% is the target for 2031 · First two-bin pilot starts in Shakhbout City, September 2026
- Source: The National (Jul 2026); Tadweer ESG Report 2025

**Slide 4, complication**
- Action title: The new plants cannot recover what arrives, because the waste comes in mixed
- Statement: Sorting plants expect to recover only 15 to 20% of what they receive as clean recyclables.
- Key facts: Years of campaigns have changed household habits little · Most towers have a single rubbish chute · Families have never made sorting part of the routine
- Source: Tadweer ESG Report 2025; Korean Ministry of Environment (2024)

### Tier 1b, the visuals

Build slide 3 as a `kpi` stat slide, not as text. These three stats are ready to use:

  - 37.7% to 80% — Waste diverted from landfill (2025 actual against the 2031 target)
  - 15-20% — Recovered clean by new plants (Of what arrives today)
  - Sept 2026 — First two-bin pilot (Shakhbout City)

- Banner line: The capacity is arriving. The clean input is not.

For slide 4, a chart carries it better than a sentence:

- Title: Only a fifth of what reaches the new plants comes back as clean recyclable
- Unit: Share of waste received
- Categories: Recovered clean, Lost to contamination
- Values: 17.5, 82.5
- Read: New plants recover 15 to 20% of what arrives · The rest is too mixed to use · Capacity is not the constraint, input quality is

Image subjects to ask for, in this order:

  - a residential tower rubbish chute
  - a two-compartment kitchen bin in use
  - a sorting line at a materials recovery facility

### Tier 2, the question

- Typical first draft: "How can we increase source-segregation of waste in communities by making it a family habit?"
- What it is missing: which community; how a habit is measured; by when; sorted well enough for the plants to use
- One SMART version: "How can we get 60% of Shakhbout City pilot households sorting every week, with under 15% contamination in the recycling bin, by December 2027?"
- S Shakhbout City pilot households · M 60% weekly, under 15% contamination · A Tadweer runs the bins, families act · R clean input for the 80% target · T December 2027

### Tier 3, the hypotheses

- H1 Sorting is easy: families can sort in under a minute a day
- H2 Sorting is worth it: families gain from sorting, or pay for not sorting
- H3 Sorting is visible: families know the rules and see the result
- MECE check: can families sort, do they want to, do they know how and see it work

### Tier 4, the model storyline

- Answer: Tadweer should make sorting a family habit in the Shakhbout pilot, then scale it
- Context: the 80% target, 37.7% in 2025, the September 2026 two-bin pilot
- Gaps: plants recover only what arrives clean, 15 to 20% expected back, nobody knows how many pilot homes sort
- Solutions: South Korea took food-waste recycling from 2% in 1995 to 97% in 2023, with pay-per-bag fees then weighing bins
- Recommendation: five habit levers in the pilot led by kids and the mosque. Kitchen bin, points, sermon, bin inspectors, "oops" tags. First step: baseline the sorting rate in pilot homes

## 02 Air quality

Group 2. Sector: Environment Quality.

### Tier 1, the brief

**Slide 3, situation**
- Action title: Fine dust in Abu Dhabi runs at over six times the WHO guideline
- Statement: PM2.5 averaged 32 micrograms per cubic metre in 2024, against a WHO annual guideline of 5.
- Key facts: 22 EAD monitoring stations, 20 fixed and 2 mobile · 40% of PM2.5 is pollution blown in from outside · 25% is desert dust
- Source: IQAir World Air Quality Report 2024; EAD Air Quality Report 2022

**Slide 4, complication**
- Action title: Nobody can say which source drives a bad day, so every fix is a guess
- Statement: The authorities cannot predict the next spike or say which neighbourhoods breathe the worst of it.
- Key facts: Greening, traffic limits and industry caps all compete · Desert dust is treated as beyond control · Residents get no warning before a spike
- Source: EAD Air Quality Report 2022 and source study; Beijing EEB (2025)

### Tier 1b, the visuals

Build slide 3 as a `kpi` stat slide, not as text. These three stats are ready to use:

  - 32 to 5 — PM2.5 micrograms per cubic metre (2024 average against the WHO guideline)
  - 22 — EAD monitoring stations (20 fixed and 2 mobile)
  - 48h — Warning residents get today (None)

- Banner line: The monitoring exists. The warning does not.

For slide 4, a chart carries it better than a sentence:

- Title: Two thirds of Abu Dhabi's fine dust comes from outside the city or the desert
- Unit: Share of PM2.5
- Categories: Blown in from outside, Desert dust, Everything else
- Values: 40, 25, 35
- Read: 40% is human-made pollution from outside · 25% is desert dust · Only the remainder is clearly local to act on

Image subjects to ask for, in this order:

  - an air quality monitoring station in open desert
  - the Abu Dhabi skyline on a dusty day
  - a classroom air purifier

### Tier 2, the question

- Typical first draft: "How can we use AI and data to reduce population exposure to PM2.5?"
- What it is missing: which population and areas; reduce by how much; by when; what the AI is for, finding sources or forecasting or warning
- One SMART version: "How can EAD use AI and data to warn residents 48 hours before 8 in 10 PM2.5 spike days, and cut exposure in the five worst-hit neighbourhoods by 20%, by December 2027?"
- S five worst-hit neighbourhoods · M 8 in 10 spikes warned, exposure down 20% · A EAD owns the stations and the data · R every intervention needs this first · T December 2027

### Tier 3, the hypotheses

- H1 We know the cause: EAD can name the source and the place of every spike
- H2 People are warned: residents hear in time and know what to do
- H3 Local sources are cut: what Abu Dhabi controls goes down
- MECE check: do we know where it comes from and who breathes it, can people avoid it, can we reduce it

### Tier 4, the model storyline

- Answer: EAD should use AI to name the source of each PM2.5 spike and warn residents 48 hours ahead
- Context: 32 micrograms in 2024, 20 fixed and 2 mobile stations, spikes peak in the summer dust season
- Gaps: no one can name the driver of a bad day, sources mix, residents get no warning
- Solutions: Beijing cut PM2.5 by 70% from 2013 to 2025, and source studies showed what to cut first, with coal falling from 22% to 3% of PM2.5
- Recommendation: five levers starting with school sensors and a 48-hour forecast. First step: trace last summer's spike days

## 03 Private climate finance

Group 3. Sector: Integrated Environment Policy & Planning.

### Tier 1, the brief

**Slide 3, situation**
- Action title: The UAE has committed $163 billion to net zero against a need near $680 billion
- Statement: Public commitment covers roughly a quarter of what the transition is estimated to cost.
- Key facts: $163 billion committed by the UAE · $680 billion is the estimated total need · Alterra aims to draw $250 billion by 2030
- Source: US State Dept (2024); Standard Chartered via BCG (2022)

**Slide 4, complication**
- Action title: Private capital holds the money but sees little reason to deploy it
- Statement: Without a carbon price or a clear return, climate projects lose to everything else on the balance sheet.
- Key facts: Public money alone cannot close a gap that size · Blame falls on immature technology and weak disclosure · Private companies hold most of the capital
- Source: CNBC (Aug 2026); UK Green Investment Bank (2015)

### Tier 1b, the visuals

Build slide 3 as a `kpi` stat slide, not as text. These three stats are ready to use:

  - $163B to $680B — Committed against total need (UAE net-zero path)
  - $30B to $250B — Alterra fund and its 2030 draw (Climate fund leverage target)
  - 4x — The size of the gap (Need against commitment)

- Banner line: Public money cannot close a gap four times its size.

For slide 4, a chart carries it better than a sentence:

- Title: Public commitment covers about a quarter of what the transition needs
- Unit: US$ billion
- Categories: Committed, Still needed
- Values: 163, 517
- Read: $163 billion committed so far · $680 billion is the estimated need · The remainder has to come from private capital

Image subjects to ask for, in this order:

  - a solar farm under construction in the desert
  - the ADGM financial district
  - a wind and battery storage site

### Tier 2, the question

- Typical first draft: "How can we incentivize private sector investment in climate action?"
- What it is missing: which investors; which projects and where; how much money; by when
- One SMART version: "How can EAD and partners mobilise AED 10 billion of private investment in Abu Dhabi climate projects, at AED 3 private for every AED 1 public, by December 2027?"
- S private investors in Abu Dhabi climate projects · M AED 10 billion, 3 to 1 · A EAD, ADGM, banks and funds act · R narrows the $680 billion gap · T December 2027

### Tier 3, the hypotheses

- H1 It pays: investors earn a return that beats the risk
- H2 It is easy: investors can find and buy deals
- H3 It is expected: rules and peers push money to climate
- MECE check: does it pay, can investors find and buy it, do rules and peers expect it

### Tier 4, the model storyline

- Answer: Abu Dhabi should take the first risk alongside private investors and give them ready projects to fund
- Context: $163 billion committed against about $680 billion needed, Alterra's $30 billion fund aiming to draw $250 billion by 2030
- Gaps: public money cannot close a gap four times its size, companies hold the capital but see no clear return, no carbon price
- Solutions: the UK Green Investment Bank drew about £3 of private money for every public £1, because public money went in first
- Recommendation: five levers led by a co-investment fund and a project pipeline. First step: list the projects ready for funding

## 04 Counting the canopy

Group 4. Sector: Environment Information, Science & Outreach.

### Tier 1, the brief

**Slide 3, situation**
- Action title: The UAE has pledged 100 million mangroves by 2030 and has planted 44 million
- Statement: Planting is running at pace, and every seedling that goes in the ground is counted.
- Key facts: 100 million mangroves pledged by 2030 · Around 44 million already in the ground · Debate focuses on species, nurseries and irrigation
- Source: The National (Dec 2023; Mar 2026); Abu Dhabi Mangrove Initiative (2023)

**Slide 4, complication**
- Action title: Planting is counted carefully, survival is barely counted at all
- Statement: At one Fujairah site only 28% of seedlings were alive after two years. The rest are unknown.
- Key facts: 28% survival after two years at one site · No survival figure exists for most sites · Faster planting is treated as the priority
- Source: The National (Mar 2026); EAD Mangrove Restoration Guidelines

### Tier 1b, the visuals

Build slide 3 as a `kpi` stat slide, not as text. These three stats are ready to use:

  - 44M to 100M — Mangroves planted against the pledge (By 2030)
  - 28% — Seedlings alive after two years (At one Fujairah site)
  - 6M — Seeds sown by drone in 2023 (Survival unmeasured)

- Banner line: Planting is counted. Survival is not.

For slide 4, a chart carries it better than a sentence:

- Title: Fewer than three in ten seedlings were alive two years after planting
- Unit: Share of seedlings at one site
- Categories: Alive after two years, Lost
- Values: 28, 72
- Read: 28% survived at the one site measured · No survival figure exists for the rest · The pledge counts planting, not trees that live

Image subjects to ask for, in this order:

  - mangrove seedlings in a nursery tray
  - a drone sowing mangrove seeds over a tidal flat
  - an aerial view of a mangrove stand

### Tier 2, the question

- Typical first draft: "How can we monitor tree inventory and plantation health across the UAE?"
- What it is missing: which sites first; what counts as healthy; how often we check; by when
- One SMART version: "How can EAD report survival for 100% of Abu Dhabi mangrove planting sites twice a year, and lift two-year survival above 50%, by December 2027?"
- S every Abu Dhabi planting site · M 100% of sites, survival above 50% · A EAD and planting partners act · R makes the 100 million pledge count · T December 2027

### Tier 3, the hypotheses

- H1 We can see every site: each site is checked from the air and on the ground
- H2 The numbers are trusted: one registry, one method, shared openly
- H3 Survival is rewarded: planting and money follow what lives
- MECE check: can we see each site, can we trust and share the numbers, does survival pay

### Tier 4, the model storyline

- Answer: EAD should count survival, not planting. Check every site by drone and AI, and pay for trees that live
- Context: 100 million pledged by 2030, about 44 million planted, drones sowed 6 million seeds in 2023
- Gaps: 28% survival at one site, no figures for the others, planting counted and survival not
- Solutions: early drone-seeding trials reached up to 48% success, and EAD guidelines already define survival as a count after summer and after winter
- Recommendation: five levers starting with AI seedling counts and one registry. First step: check last year's sites this winter

## 05 Ecotourism

Group 5. Sector: Terrestrial & Marine Biodiversity.

### Tier 1, the brief

**Slide 3, situation**
- Action title: Abu Dhabi plans to grow from 26.6 to 39.3 million visitors by 2030
- Statement: The growth plan leans on hotels, attractions and campaigns for culture and heritage.
- Key facts: 26.6 million visitors in 2025 · 39.3 million is the target for 2030 · Conservationists warn fragile sites cannot take the load
- Source: DCT Abu Dhabi (2025-26)

**Slide 4, complication**
- Action title: World-class islands, mangroves and deserts sit barely visited
- Statement: A 200-island guide and an Al Dhafra plan exist, yet nature is rarely why people come.
- Key facts: 200 islands in the new water sports guide · Costa Rica protects 25% of its land · 60% of Costa Rica is forest
- Source: Abu Dhabi Islands and Water Sports Guide (2026); World Bank (2023)

### Tier 1b, the visuals

Build slide 3 as a `kpi` stat slide, not as text. These three stats are ready to use:

  - 26.6M to 39.3M — Visitors to Abu Dhabi (2025 actual against the 2030 target)
  - 200 — Islands in the new guide (Barely visited)
  - 25% — Of Costa Rica is protected land (The comparison case)

- Banner line: The visitors are coming. Nature is not why.

For slide 4, a chart carries it better than a sentence:

- Title: Abu Dhabi plans to add almost 13 million visitors in five years
- Unit: Visitors, millions
- Categories: 2025 actual, 2030 target
- Values: 26.6, 39.3
- Read: 26.6 million visitors in 2025 · 39.3 million is the 2030 target · Fragile sites absorb the growth either way

Image subjects to ask for, in this order:

  - kayaking through mangroves at low tide
  - a desert camp at dusk in Al Dhafra
  - an empty island beach in the Arabian Gulf

### Tier 2, the question

- Typical first draft: "How can we activate ecotourism?"
- What it is missing: which visitors; which places and experiences; how success is measured; how the sites are protected; by when
- One SMART version: "How can Abu Dhabi get 1 in 10 visitors to book a nature experience, with no damage to protected sites, by December 2027?"
- S visitors to Abu Dhabi, nature trips · M 1 in 10 visitors book one · A DCT, EAD and operators act · R helps reach 39.3 million by 2030 · T December 2027

### Tier 3, the hypotheses

- H1 There is something to do: nature trips worth the journey, run by local people
- H2 Visitors know and can book: nature is in the plan and easy to book
- H3 Visits protect the sites: limits hold and visits pay for conservation
- MECE check: is there something to do, do visitors know and can they book, do visits protect the sites

### Tier 4, the model storyline

- Answer: Abu Dhabi should package its nature into bookable trips, sold when people choose where to travel
- Context: 26.6 million visitors in 2025 against a 39.3 million target, a new guide covering 200 islands
- Gaps: islands, mangroves and deserts barely visited, nature a footnote in why people come, fragile sites at risk
- Solutions: Costa Rica protects 25% of its land and drew 2.9 million visitors and $5.6 billion in 2025, paying landowners to keep forest standing
- Recommendation: five levers starting with kayak trails and one booking app. First step: count nature visitors this winter

## Notes on the targets

Every number in a SMART question above is a suggested target from the facilitator, not
an EAD commitment. If a group adopts one, say so on the slide. If they set their own,
better: ask them to say why that level and not another.
